# RadonVerdict SEO topic expansion

**Date:** 2026-09-05  
**Market:** United States, English-language organic search  
**Decision:** Expand from five test-protocol pages to a 20-page problem/answer library, while keeping health-risk and system-design claims out of the publishing lane.

## Direct conclusion

The five-page cluster was too small. It covered the center of the testing workflow but missed the concrete questions people search while a test or mitigation system is already in progress. The expanded library now contains 20 indexable guides grouped into test setup, test conditions, test handling, retesting, and system maintenance.

The opportunity is not the broad query `radon`. Government domains dominate broad testing and health explanations. The more realistic entry point is a set of narrow operational searches where current results include local contractors, thin FAQs, forums, and newly published independent guides.

## Evidence used

- Stored Search Console evidence: the historical `/radon-levels` family produced 91 clicks / 6,416 impressions and the site previously reached 261 clicks / 23,290 impressions in a 28-day period. This proves prior topical eligibility, not future ranking.
- Live SERP samples checked on 2026-09-05 for placement, duration, windows, occupancy, invalid tests, weather, device choice, expired kits, retesting, manometers, fan noise, maintenance, and fan life.
- Primary claims checked against [CDC home testing guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/radon/testing/index.html), [EPA's home-sale testing guide](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-05/documents/hmbuygud.pdf), [EPA retesting guidance](https://www.epa.gov/radon/how-often-should-i-testretest-my-home-radon), and [EPA mitigation-system maintenance guidance](https://www.epa.gov/radon/how-do-i-know-if-my-radon-mitigation-system-working-properly).
- Google Ads Keyword Planner was queried through the authenticated Ads account on 2026-09-05. The visible ad-blocker warning did not prevent the underlying planner from running. The figures below use United States targeting, English, Google, and the previous 12 months (2025-08 through 2026-07).

- Direct conclusion: Google already reads RadonVerdict primarily as a testing/local-data site. RadonConnect exposes a broader commercial mitigation footprint. The recovery move is therefore not “publish more generic radon facts”; it is to connect our testing and county evidence to the existing cost, quote, contractor, system-maintenance, and post-mitigation decision paths.

### Publishing priority after Ads validation

1. Pillars and commercial bridges: test/planner, test kit/device choice, mitigation system, cost, quote, contractor decision support.
2. Proven task demand: test duration, result meaning, levels chart, monitor/detector, maintenance, fan life, manometer, fan noise.
3. Supporting protocol pages: placement, closed-house conditions, retesting, apartment testing, season.
4. Zero-band exact phrasings: keep indexable for now, link them from stronger hubs, and consolidate if they fail to earn Search Console impressions.

### What the sample changes

1. The site does **not** have one universal SEO competitor. Each query has a different result class: government, public-interest organization, local contractor, manufacturer, affiliate publisher, or forum.
2. Broad authority queries such as testing and result thresholds require EPA/CDC-compatible answers plus a genuinely better workflow. Rewording the same facts will not be enough.
3. The most attainable national openings are operational queries where local contractors and Reddit rank: manometer readings, fan life/noise, maintenance records, test timing, and quote decomposition.
4. Product-review intent is commercially attractive but demands evidence we do not yet have. Publishing “best kit” recommendations without testing or a defensible comparison protocol would add both trust and YMYL risk.
5. Every priority page should be reverse-engineered against its own top five URLs before major rewriting. Page count is not the unit of progress; closing a specific SERP utility gap is.

### Page anatomy observed

- **CDC testing page:** concise key points; “when to test,” device types, placement, duration, result bands, and next actions on one page; current reviewed/updated date and explicit subject-matter review. It wins with authority and completeness, but it does not separate sampling time from shipping/lab/report turnaround or give users a reusable record.
- **Inspection Professionals duration page:** exact-match H1, author/date, immediate intent framing, short-term vs. long-term section, a 48-hour answer, a three-to-five-day total timeline, delay factors, transaction context, five FAQs, and a service CTA. Its weakness is that several rules are presented broadly from a local service perspective instead of being tied line-by-line to device or jurisdictional sources.
- **EPA maintenance page:** extremely short but authoritative. It covers warning-device checks, fan life, replacement cost, and two-year retesting in a few paragraphs. This leaves substantial utility space for a sourced maintenance calendar and record system without contradicting EPA.
- **Utah Radon manometer page:** under three minutes, one real image, immediate uneven-vs.-level interpretation, a zero-reading branch, and a request-a-bid CTA. It ranks despite being thin. Its numerical change thresholds are not clearly sourced, creating an opening for RadonVerdict's safer baseline-comparison approach.
- **Peerless cost page:** named operations author, fresh publish/update dates, an immediate national range, system-type price table, cost drivers, operating expense, fan replacement, quote-comparison advice, FAQs, related cluster links, calculator, and service conversion. This is the strongest commercial template in the sample.
- **Bob Vila kit page:** named testers, original photos, hands-on testing statement, comparison table, category winners, specifications, scored ratings, pros/cons, methodology, and affiliate disclosure. This establishes the minimum evidence bar for a serious `best radon test kit` attack; a source-only rewrite would not be competitive.

### Competitors worth watching

- [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/radon/testing/index.html) and [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/radon): authority leaders. We should translate their rules into a workflow, not rewrite their general explanations.
- [RadonConnect](https://radonconnect.com/tools/): product/tool competitor with a broad utility footprint.
- RadonZoneReport, Ready Home Systems, and InspectandTest: 2026 independent publishers using direct answers, updated dates, source blocks, comparisons, and conversion paths. Their freshness means the cluster is actively contested.
- Local inspectors and mitigation contractors: often rank with one-question FAQs because the intent is specific and current national answers are uneven.
- Reddit and home-improvement forums: repeated appearance is a discovery signal that users are not satisfied by generic government prose. Forum claims are not used as factual sources.

## Measurement rule

Do not call the expansion successful because 20 URLs exist. After indexing, export Search Console query/page data weekly and watch non-branded impressions, clicks, CTR, and cannibalization. Consolidate pages when two URLs repeatedly appear for the same query. Expand a subtopic only after it earns impressions or Keyword Planner shows meaningful adjacent demand.

Keyword Planner now supplies the first demand baseline. Recheck the same United States/English keyword set monthly, but use Search Console query/page data as the deciding evidence for whether the zero-band support pages remain separate or are consolidated. Do not read the apparent `+900%` change on `radon mitigation system maintenance` as durable growth without a second observation; a small prior base can exaggerate the percentage.
